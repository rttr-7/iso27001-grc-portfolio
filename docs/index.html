Taking a payments company from no security management system to ISO/IEC 27001 certification in 11 months

The problem

Security existed, but no one could prove it was managed

Halcyon Ledger, a fictional 210-person payments company, had security tools but no risk owners, no measurements and no evidence an auditor could rely on. Three enterprise customers refused to sign without ISO 27001, leaving £4.2m of annual revenue on hold.

What we did

Built and tested an ISMS in five steps

What we achieved

Certified on 16 October 2026

60-second summary

I built an ISO 27001 case study for a simulated payments company called Halcyon Ledger, which needed certification to win large customers. First I defined what the security system covers: the whole HalcyonPay product, both offices, the cloud and key suppliers. Then I ran a risk assessment and found 12 significant risks, such as fraudsters changing bank details or ransomware. For each one I chose controls and named an owner, which brought every risk within appetite. I went through all 93 Annex A controls and recorded which apply and why in the Statement of Applicability. Next I ran an internal audit, found 3 minor nonconformities, identified the root cause of each and corrected them. Finally, leadership reviewed the results and approved going for certification.

Halcyon Ledger Ltd is a fictional organisation. All names, figures and findings are simulated to demonstrate how ISO/IEC 27001 is applied in practice.

Step 1 · Clauses 4.1, 4.2, 4.3

The ISMS covers the entire HalcyonPay service

Purpose

The scope defines what the ISMS protects: which services, locations, people and systems are included.

What ISO 27001 requires

The organisation must understand its context and stakeholders, and document a scope with its boundaries and dependencies. The scope appears on the certificate.

Say it like this

"We covered everything customers rely on, not just one office. A narrow scope is easier to certify but tells customers nothing."

Step 2 · Clauses 6.1.2, 6.1.3, 8.2, 8.3

Six risks exceeded appetite; treatment brought all of them within it

Purpose

Identify what could harm the confidentiality, integrity or availability of information, score it, and decide how to treat it.

What ISO 27001 requires

A defined, repeatable risk assessment process with acceptance criteria, a treatment plan, and risk owners' approval of residual risk.

Say it like this

"We found 12 significant risks. Six scored 12 or more and had to be treated. After treatment, none were above that line."

Scoring method

Likelihood 1–5×Impact 1–5=Risk score 1–25
15–25 · CriticalTreat immediately and report to the board. Only the CEO can accept.
12–14 · HighTreat within 90 days. Executive sign-off required.
7–10 · MediumTreat within 6 months, or accept with written justification.
1–6 · LowAcceptable. Monitor.

Step 3 · Clause 6.1.3 d · Annex A

91 of 93 Annex A controls apply; 2 are excluded with evidence

Purpose

The Statement of Applicability (SoA) lists all 93 Annex A controls and records whether each applies, why, and its implementation status.

What ISO 27001 requires

Compare selected controls with Annex A so none are overlooked, and justify every inclusion and exclusion.

Say it like this

"We use 91 of 93 controls, each linked to a risk, a law, a contract or good practice. We excluded two, and can prove why."

Step 4 · Clause 9.2 · ISO 19011

No major nonconformities; three minor findings, all corrected

Purpose

An internal audit checks the ISMS against ISO 27001 and the organisation's own rules before the certification body arrives.

What ISO 27001 requires

A planned audit programme, objective auditors, and reported results. Nonconformities need root cause analysis and corrective action.

Say it like this

"No major findings and three minor ones. For each, we found the root cause and fixed it so it won't recur."

Major nonconformityA requirement is not met at all, or the system is failing.
Minor nonconformityAn isolated lapse. The system still works.
Opportunity for improvementThe requirement is met, but could be met better.

Step 5 · Clause 9.3 · 11 August 2026

Top management judged the ISMS effective and approved certification

Purpose

Top management reviews objectives, risks, audit results and stakeholder feedback, then decides on changes and resources.

What ISO 27001 requires

Reviews at planned intervals covering the inputs in 9.3.2 (a–g), with documented decisions on improvement and changes.

Say it like this

"The CEO and directors reviewed everything, agreed the system works, and funded the last two treatments."
